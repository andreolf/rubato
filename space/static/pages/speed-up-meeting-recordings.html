Why meetings gain the most

A scripted talk has little slack. A meeting has a lot: people joining, "can you see my screen", waiting for a page to load, someone thinking out loud. The parts you need when catching up are short and scattered: what was decided, who is doing what, the dates and the figures.

What plays at which speed

In the meetingSpeed
"So the decision is", "three action items", "to summarise", dates, amounts, percentages1.0x
Ordinary discussion1.2x
"Can you hear me", "give me a second", "let me share my screen"1.5x
Silence with nothing happening on screen2.0x

All four speeds are adjustable. For a call you only need the gist of, raise the ordinary speed to 1.5x and the silence to 3x.

Three ways to do it

1. In the browser

Upload the recording, up to {{MAX_MINUTES}} minutes. The upload is deleted when processing ends and the result after 30 minutes. Suitable for calls that are not sensitive.

2. On your own computer

For long or confidential calls. Nothing leaves your machine and there is no length limit.

uvx --from git+https://github.com/andreolf/rubato rubato auto standup.mp4

3. Through Claude or another agent

Install the skill and ask the agent to speed up the recording. It reads the transcript first, so it can keep the real decisions at normal speed even when nobody said the word "decision", and it can tell you what it slowed down for.

npx skills add andreolf/rubato

Compared with a meeting summary

An AI summary is quicker to read, and it is one reader's account of what mattered. When the detail counts, such as who agreed to what or how a number was qualified, you want the recording itself. A dynamic-speed version is the recording, complete, in less time. The two work well together: read the summary, then watch the passages around the decisions.

Limits

  • Speakers are not identified; the speed depends on what is said, not who says it.
  • The built-in phrases are English. For calls in other languages, use an agent to mark the passages.
  • Heavy crosstalk transcribes poorly, so fewer passages are recognised.
