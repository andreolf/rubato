Where the time in a lecture goes

A recorded lecture is not dense from start to finish. A good part of it is the lecturer finding the right slide, waiting for a question, repeating a point for the back row, or writing on the board in silence. Those minutes are safe to speed up a lot. The definition, the worked example and "there are three cases" are not.

A flat 1.5x or 2x treats both the same, so you end up rewinding exactly where it matters.

The method

  1. Get the recording as a file. Most lecture platforms let you download the video; a screen recording works too.
  2. Run it through Rubato. It transcribes the lecture and gives each passage a speed.
  3. Watch the new file in any player. You can still use your player's speed control on top.
In the lectureSpeed
"The key idea is", "there are three cases", a formula read out, a result with numbers1.0x
Explanation and examples1.2x
"Can everyone see this", "let me find the slide", restarts1.5x
Silence with nothing changing on screen2.0x

Silence while the lecturer is writing or a slide is building is not treated as dead air, because something is happening on screen.

For a full-length lecture

The online demo is limited to {{MAX_MINUTES}} minutes. For an hour or more, run it on your own computer, where there is no limit and the file never leaves your machine:

uvx --from git+https://github.com/andreolf/rubato rubato auto lecture.mp4

If you already watch at 1.5x, keep that as your ordinary speed and let the rest scale around it:

uvx --from git+https://github.com/andreolf/rubato rubato auto lecture.mp4 --base 1.5 --fast 2 --silence 3

Let an AI agent pick the passages

Phrase matching only finds takeaways that are announced. Lecturers often do not announce them. If you use Claude, Cursor or another agent, install Rubato as a skill and ask it to speed up the lecture: the agent reads the whole transcript and marks the real definitions, results and tangents before the video is rendered.

npx skills add andreolf/rubato

What to expect

How much time you save depends on the lecture. With the default speeds, the 54-second sample talk on the home page comes out at 44 seconds, about what a flat 1.2x gives, with the passages that matter untouched. Raising the ordinary speed saves more. A tightly scripted lecture has less to give back than a live, improvised one.
