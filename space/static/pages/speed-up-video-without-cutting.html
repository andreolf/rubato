What cutting does

Silence removers find the stretches where the audio is quiet and delete them. Jump-cut editors do the same thing with more control. The result is tighter, and it is the standard look of a lot of online video.

The costs come from the deletion:

  • The picture jumps at every cut. Fine for a talking head, distracting for a screen recording or a slide deck.
  • Anything that happened during the quiet is gone. A demo loading, a slide building, a diagram being drawn.
  • Subtitles and timestamps no longer match the original.
  • Only silence is affected. Filler that is spoken aloud stays at full length.

What changing speed does

Variable speed keeps every frame and changes how long each one is shown. Quiet stretches play at 2x or more, spoken filler a little faster, and the important passages at normal speed. The video stays continuous, and because nothing is removed, every moment of the original maps to a moment in the result, so subtitles can be retimed exactly.

When to use which

Cut when you are the editor and the goal is a finished piece: a vlog, a short, a course video where pace is part of the style.

Change speed when the recording is a record: a lecture, a meeting, a talk, a tutorial someone else made. You want all of it, in less time, without deciding what to throw away.

Doing it

Upload a file in the browser, connect Rubato in Claude, or run it locally:

uvx --from git+https://github.com/andreolf/rubato rubato auto recording.mp4

To preview what would change before rendering anything, add --plan-only. If you want one constant speed with no variation, --flat 1.25 does that.
